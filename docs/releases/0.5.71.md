# Kuvo Code 0.5.71 — the panes take the Mac's terrain

- **Three cards, one terrain.** The sessions, the conversation and the split now share the
  Mac's own `PaneLayout` (`MainView.swift:2112-2179`): the sessions are drawn 220–440 pt
  wide, the panel 300–900, the split 380–1200, and the conversation always keeps at least
  380 whatever else is on screen. The page measures the window and hands the layout what
  each pane asks for and what is shown, and the three widths are written back to the page —
  so a window that grows gives the cards the widths it remembers.
- **Out of room, they give way in the Mac's order.** The split goes first, then the panel,
  then the sessions. A split the window cannot hold is put aside rather than drawn over the
  conversation, and comes back by itself when the room does; a pane that went early is
  restored if a later one's going left room for it. If room is still short, the rest shrink
  as far as they must — the panel first and the split last.
- **Every card can be dragged, and stops where the Mac stops.** The handles between the
  cards are the Mac's shape: zero width in the gap, their grab area reaching over both
  cards, and the accent hairline while pointed at or dragged. Each drag stays inside the
  pane's own range and inside the room the conversation leaves (`PaneResizer`).

213 unit tests pass — the layout's own among them, proved without a window in
`windows/harness/layout.mjs` — `tsc --noEmit` is clean, the page builds, and the live page
against the real engine passes in Chromium: a second session is made, the split opens on the
first with its own header and rows, Swap exchanges them, the empty session asks its question
beside, and at 900 pt the split gives way while at 1280 it comes back.

Windows 10/11, x64. The installer and the portable build are unsigned; Windows may warn on
first run.
